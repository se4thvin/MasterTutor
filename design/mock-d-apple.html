Choosing the learning rate

Your learning algorithm will run much better with an appropriate choice of learning rate. If it's too small, it will run very slowly, and if it's too large, it may not even converge.

If you plot the cost for a number of iterations and notice that the cost sometimes goes up and sometimes goes down, you should take that as a clear sign that gradient descent is not working properly.

This could mean there's a bug in the code, or sometimes it could mean that your learning rate is too large.

    Learning-rate warmup, explained

    Mira Okafor · March 14, 2026 · 9 min read

    Training a transformer with Adam at its full learning rate from step zero often diverges within the first few thousand steps. Warmup, a short ramp from near-zero to the peak rate, is the cheapest fix we know, and the reason it works is less obvious than it looks.

    The update rule

    Adam scales each step by a running estimate of the gradient's second moment. Early in training that estimate rests on only a handful of samples, so the effective step size can be enormous for parameters whose gradients happen to start small:

    θt+1 = θt − ηt · m̂t / (√v̂t + ε)

    Learning-rate warmup, explained.

    Mira Okafor · March 14, 2026 · 9 min read · 14 blocks

    Training a transformer with Adam at its full learning rate from step zero often diverges within the first few thousand steps. Warmup, a short ramp from near-zero to the peak rate, is the cheapest fix we know, and the reason it works is less obvious than it looks.

    The update rule

    Adam scales each step by a running estimate of the gradient's second moment. Early in training that estimate rests on only a handful of samples, so the effective step size can be enormous for parameters whose gradients happen to start small:

    θt+1 = θt − ηt · m̂t√v̂t + ε(1)

    Common schedules

    ScheduleWarmupDecayTypical use

    Inverse square root4,000 steps∝ 1/√tOriginal Transformer (2017)
    Warmup-stable-decay1% of stepslinear, final 10%Continual pretraining
    ConstantnonenoneSmall fine-tunes

    In code

    import math

def lr_at(step, max_lr=3e-4, warmup=2_000, total=100_000):
    if step < warmup:
        return max_lr * step / warmup
    progress = (step - warmup) / (total - warmup)
    return max_lr * (0.1 + 0.45 * (1 + math.cos(math.pi * progress)))
